Target Architecture & Official Grading Rubric

Specification for all five operational targets.

T1: Motor Temperature (°C) KNN vs Ridge vs HistGBM

Predicts component temperature. Evaluated on both Permanent Magnet rotor (pm) and Stator Winding (stator_winding). Ridge achieves 4.76 °C MAE; HistGBM achieves 6.45 °C MAE on unseen test profiles.

T2: Time to Overheating (s) HistGBM Time-to-Threshold Model

Determines when temperature will cross safety threshold. Trained on 64,841 extracted time-series crossing labels. Achieves R² = 0.7508 with MAE of 122.6 s (2.0 minutes) on unseen test profiles.

T3: Cooling Start Time (s) Rule-Based Predictive Control

Supervisory control rule: T3 = max(0, T2 - 60s). Triggers active cooling 60 seconds before overheating is reached. Labeled as a control-policy demonstration.

T4: Cooling Duration (s) First-Order Thermal-Response Model

Calculates duration required to pull temperature to target: T4 = R_th * C_th * ln((T_start - T_inf_cool)/(T_target - T_inf_cool)). Yields 778.4 s (13.0 min) for nominal parameters.

T5: Final Temperature after Cooling (°C) First-Order Thermal-Response Model

Calculates expected temperature at cooling shut-off and tracks reheating to the end of the 30-minute horizon: Peak = 68.5 °C (safe), Shut-off = 65.5 °C, End = 71.9 °C.

The "KNN Coolant Paradox" & Physical Monotonicity

Why unconstrained Machine Learning fails counterfactual cooling tests and how monotonicity resolves it.

The Problem: Spurious Correlation in Passive Bench Data

In test-bench datasets, coolant fluid is passive: it naturally heats up when the motor is worked hard. As a result, pure distance-based models (KNN) learn a spurious correlation where low coolant temperature implies idle operating conditions.

When simulating an active cooling intervention (manually dropping coolant by 15 °C), pure KNN looks up nearest neighbors from idle cycles and erroneously predicts that the motor heats up faster.

The Solution: Monotonic Gradient Boosting

We enforce a Monotonic Constraint on the coolant feature in HistGradientBoostingRegressor (monotonic_cst = +1).

This mathematically guarantees that the partial derivative of temperature increment with respect to coolant is non-negative. Colder coolant is guaranteed to strictly reduce or hold steady the heating rate.
